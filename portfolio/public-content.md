# Public content (verbal family)

Public company and product pages explain in this order. Plain English first.
Technical depth second. A visitor must understand the first half of a page
without platform-engineering vocabulary. A Staff/Principal engineer must still
find true mechanism and proof further down.

## Layers (required on dedicated product sites)

1. **Outcome** — what gets better?
2. **Problem** — what is difficult today?
3. **Product** — what does this product actually do?
4. **Workflow** — how would someone use it?
5. **Mechanism** — how does it accomplish that?
6. **Proof** — what capabilities or evidence exist (honest class)?
7. **System** — how it relates to sibling products (not a suite requirement)
8. **Action** — truthful next step given maturity

Company `opsdevco.de` uses the same voice. It is not a ninth copy of a product
page. Company layers are: system problem → what OpsDevCode provides → four
products → how they compose → approach → action.

## Audience tests

Every first-layer headline and paragraph must pass both:

- **Senior application engineer:** understandable without being a platform engineer.
- **Staff platform engineer:** still technically true.

Fail either → rewrite. Do not lead with “AI-powered.” Do not market Relay.
Do not make Convergence a product.

## Capability claims

Per capability, public copy may only use: `available`, `preview`,
`in-development`, `planned`. Do not present roadmap as shipped. Do not
fabricate customers, metrics, or UI.

## Site ownership

- Company repo: `opsdevco.de` and `/products/{slug}` (portfolio context).
- Product repos: dedicated sites on `{product}.opsdevco.de`.
- Waitlist may stay on Repave until a product has its own action.
